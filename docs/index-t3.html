NRS³-Minkowski on T³ — the uncertainty cone of a closed axis

On T³ each axis is a ring of d ≥ 3 positions with the pair T° (ring adjacency / 2) and P (centred position). A state ψ gives two fluctuation vectors in ℂd; their Gram matrix is a Hermitian 2×2 matrix, and that is a vector of Minkowski space (van der Waerden 1929). Robertson–Schrödinger says it never leaves the light cone. Each axis fixes its own C°(d) — the light speed of that field is its maximal current — and everything below is solved on it: in the band the vector stays strictly inside, and the light share β² = 1 / C°² is what the state keeps of the cone while the rest, 1 − β², is dark. Every number is computed live from the matrices; every claim is a Lean theorem (table at the end).

Units are a boost

A → λA, B → B/λ keeps [A, B] and slides the vector along its hyperbola v₀² − v₃² = (‖x‖‖y‖)². The interval, the covariance and the commutator do not move: that is why C° has no units.

Step 1, seen: the two vectors site by site

Each site j carries xj (blue) and yj (orange) as arrows in the complex plane. β = |⟪x, y⟫| / (‖x‖‖y‖) = cos θNRS: the two vectors are parallel only when β = 1.

Random walk of the state: how the light decays

Press ✦ Random walk of the state: ψ takes small random steps on the unit sphere of ℂd starting where you are. Blue: the light share β² = |⟪x, y⟫|² / (‖x‖²‖y‖²); orange: the dark share 1 − β² (they always add to 1). Red bands: the state left the band |t| > 1. At the maximal current state of d = 3 the light is 4/5; for large d it goes to 1/2.

The speed of the vector at the maximal current state: β(d) = 1 / C°(d)

Timelike for every d ≥ 3 (only the saturating state of d = 3 is null). β(3) = √(4/5) = 0.8944, β(4) = 0.6495, β(5) = 0.7115 … it goes to 1/√2 = 0.7071 (the Szegő limit of the ring) from both sides: it is not monotone, so there is no floor and ceiling as on the open chain. Dashed: β², the light share.

Three closed axes, one determinant: det|NRS³| at the maximal current state of T³

On T³ each axis is a ring with its own C°. Robertson 1934 for the six observables gives det Σ / |det Ω| = C°(dx)² C°(dy)² C°(dz)² at the maximal current state (5/4 per axis at d = 3, so 125/64 on T³), and it is strict for every state with the three axes in the band.
